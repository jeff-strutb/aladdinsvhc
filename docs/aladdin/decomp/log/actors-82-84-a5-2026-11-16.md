## A wait routine that ends its actor past its exit level

KindA1Touch ($84:A9C7) is called by JSL from its kind's stub, which the
actor loop calls by JSR. To end its actor it pulls five bytes (its JSL's
return and the stub's JSR's) and jumps to ActorEndPlaced, so S is two
above its exit level at the jump. A5 ends a call at its return address, at
an RTS or RTL that takes S above the exit level, or at a jump to the loop
or an actor entry with S at the exit level: none applies, so the original's
call runs on while the C has left. rand_pw7_1 calls 261 and 324 and
rand_pw7_2 call 261: C-waits-where-original-returns. Its C is held
(`heldc: keep`); an oracle rule for a jump out with S above the exit level
would release it. Kind27Wait ($83:A012) pulls only its own return and is
judged identical.

## An actor resumed somewhere other than where it yielded

GuardChase ($A5:F0F4) sends the guard on by writing its resume address
($03) to $E5B6, $E5E8, $E6A0 or $E794: the loop's RTI resumes the guard
there, not after its last BRK. The oracles open a piece only where a
parked actor resumes at its own resume address, or at an actor entry
reached by a jump; the guard's states ($A5:E5B6, $E6A0) cannot be judged
until they model a rewritten resume address. ActorRestartIfPlayerFarLeft
($A5:EF83) is different and works: it replaces its own return address, so
its C ends unwound at the restart point ($A5:EFC5 in the scripts).

## Coverage

A table entry no script reads keeps its routine below T3 (Kind21Start,
$A5:F309: part 2 at $A5:F3D1). Leaving the entry in the intake would end the
routine's filed span there and drop the rest from the coverage count, so it
stays filed and T2. Kind $74's controller ($84:87D1), the window effects'
table ($82:8000) and the guard's other states have untraced branches inside
and wait on coverage scripts.
