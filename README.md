## Aladdin

A bit-exact decompilation of Aladdin (USA) for the Super NES: symbolic,
commented 65816 and SPC700 assembly that rebuilds the ROM byte for byte,
and C for every routine, proven behaviour exact against the running
original. The C is written as C: locals, loops and tables, setting only the
registers and flags the original leaves visible at its exit, at a yield or
to a callee (`src/aladdin/include/native.h`). The sound driver's C also makes
the SPC700's memory accesses in the original's order, as lockstep requires.
The ROM is not included; `reference/aladdin/` holds your own copy.

Standard, layout and tools: [docs/aladdin/DECOMP.md](docs/aladdin/DECOMP.md).
Plan and status: [docs/aladdin/PLAN.md](docs/aladdin/PLAN.md). Findings:
[docs/aladdin/HANDBOOK.md](docs/aladdin/HANDBOOK.md).

## The native game: Aladdin Remastered

`native/aladdin` is the game on [nativeshvc](https://github.com/jeff-strutb/nativeshvc),
a native engine, with Linux as the first platform. Each `update` is one 60 Hz
frame of the original's work; `draw` describes the picture from the game's
state. The game's C reaches the engine only through its calls (`nshvc_*`): no
register-shaped state, no translation layer and no imitated PPU, DMA, sound
chip or bus. The engine runs no processor instructions. The plan, its
decisions and its progress are in
[docs/aladdin/native/PLAN.md](docs/aladdin/native/PLAN.md); building it is in
[docs/aladdin/native/BUILD.md](docs/aladdin/native/BUILD.md).

The decomp's C (`src/aladdin`) is the certified record of what each routine
does. The native game is written from it, routine by routine, as ordinary
typed C: named state in place of memory addresses, explicit states in place
of routines that pause, and typed readers in place of cartridge addresses.
The game's logic is the original's, so a wrong value or a misplaced actor is
a bug; recordings of the original are kept to find such bugs.

### Faults found by playing and by the check, and fixed

Each was found by a play by keys or by the field-by-field check against the original, then fixed; the evidence is in
`NATIVE-FAULTS.txt` and `NATIVE-ACTOR-CHECK.txt`.

- Jafar's first form (level 16) stopped at its first random pick: `RandomFromList` took the list's X where the native game wants
  X / 2 (`56e4efad`; the same slip in kind $A1/$A6's bounce list and the chain's two picks).
- Level 3's scene (Jasmine's escape) never ran: `PlayerSceneJumpIn` and `PlayerSceneWalkOut` had been left unconverted
  on the record's word that no level plays them (`c5a384cb`).
- Kind $68's timing: a wait, a speed, a frame after each pause, and an overlapped byte (`22cc5db9`, `c4b4e918`).
- Kind $4E's spring left +$41 at 6 where the original leaves 5 (`b4edf6c2`, exact on `cov_kind4e_bounce`).
- Aladdin's top speed cap (`486c4ed7`; the demo's frames 10031 to 10158 then match in every field) and the original's
  16-bit ledge quirk (`25d29f04`, later left out as departure 1).
- The ending's stage 8 killed the player: the work-RAM clear at a game start kept stage 7's scroll kind, the original
  clears its whole range (82 fields it missed); the ending play-back now runs stages 4 to 11 with no death.
- An "ACTION" text drawn on the play field of two levels was not a fault: it is the original's own blinking text task.

## The Decompilation Standard

The purpose of a decompilation, as opposed to a recompilation, is
preservation: recovering the game itself, as written, in a form that outlives
the original hardware and media. Playability and portability are welcome side
effects, not the direct goal. This game was written by hand in assembly, so
there is no original compiler to match. The standard has two proofs instead:
the symbolic assembly must rebuild the original ROM byte for byte, and every
routine's C must give exactly the original's outputs for its inputs, checked
call by call and again with whole play sessions run in step with the original.
A bit-exact match is 100% reliability that the source is accurate to the
original, ensuring preservation for future generations.

## Project Purpose

A decompilation of **Aladdin** (SNES, 1993): every routine rewritten as
readable C, documented with what it does and how, filed by what it is for,
and proven to behave exactly like the original; and the whole ROM rebuilt byte
for byte. Progress is measured only by those two proofs; playability is a
consequence, never a reason to reorder the work.

## Game background

Capcom released *Aladdin* for the Super NES in 1993, a platform game based on
Disney's 1992 film. It is a different game from the Aladdin made for the Sega
Genesis the same year. The cartridge is 1.25 MB of ROM with no coprocessor and
no cartridge RAM. The game runs on two processors: the SNES CPU (a 65816) runs
the game logic, and the SPC700 sound CPU plays the music and effects from a
program the main CPU uploads. The retail ROM has no compiler footprint: both
programs were written by hand in assembly. Its tasks and actors are
coroutines that wait in the middle of a routine and resume there on a later
frame ([docs/aladdin/TASKS.md](docs/aladdin/TASKS.md),
[docs/aladdin/ACTORS.md](docs/aladdin/ACTORS.md)).
